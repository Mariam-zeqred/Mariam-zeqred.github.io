Queen-bee incubator: thermal control simulation

An illustrative model of a heated cabinet like the queen-bee incubator, built to show why heater control is hard on a plant with thermal lag. The parameters and controller settings are generic round numbers, not the product's. Change the sliders while it runs, open the door, inject a sensor fault, and compare controllers.

This is a teaching model with illustrative parameters. It shows the qualitative behaviour (lag, overshoot, recovery after a door opening) and does not reproduce any real device. The door-opening loss (×10) and the sensor-fault behaviour are illustrative.

What is simulated

Plant. The default is a "heater-lag" model: the heat delivered by the element, E, lags the relay with time constant τh, and the chamber loses heat to ambient: dE/dt = (a·u − E)/τ_h, dT/dt = E − b·(T − T_out). The alternative is a first-order model with a dead time. Both use round illustrative numbers. The lag is the point: after several minutes of heating the chamber keeps warming after the heater switches off, which is why a plain thermostat overshoots on this kind of plant.

Controllers. "On/off with anticipation" is a textbook anticipating thermostat: it switches on a projected temperature (the reading plus the recent rate of rise times a lead time) and turns the heater off a little before the top of the band, with a minimum OFF time and a forced rest after a long ON period. The plain thermostat only uses the band. The PID is a PI tuned with Skogestad's SIMC rules on the first-order plant, turned into relay pulses by time-proportioning. All settings are illustrative defaults, not the product firmware's.

Safety task. The interlock sits outside every controller: the heater is off while the door is open; a reading at or above 40 °C latches the heater off until an operator acknowledges and the chamber has cooled; and when the sensor stops delivering valid readings the heater is forced off and a sensor error is shown.

What to try. Start cold with controller A = on/off with anticipation and B = plain thermostat and watch the overshoot difference on the first approach. Set the anticipation and lead to zero to see it degrade into the plain thermostat. Raise the target and watch the duty cycle climb. Open the door at steady state and watch the recovery. Scale the heater power up to 1.5 and watch the cutoff latch.
