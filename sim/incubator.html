This is a simplified model. Its three parameters were fitted on one device and one day of telemetry (3 October 2026, 1 367 readings at 15 s), so it reproduces that day's steady state and duty cycle but has not been validated on new data. The door-opening loss (×10) and the sensor-fault behaviour are illustrative.

What is simulated

Plant. The default is the "heater-lag" model: the heat delivered by the element, E, lags the relay with time constant τh, and the chamber loses heat to ambient: dE/dt = (a·u − E)/τ_h, dT/dt = E − b·(T − T_out) with a = 0.59 °C/min, τ_h = 4.9 min, b = 0.0111 /min (chamber time constant 90 min). The alternative is a first-order model with a 210 s dead time (a = 0.55, b = 0.0101). Both were fitted by simulating the model against the measured trace. The lag is the main story of the real data: after several minutes of heating the chamber keeps warming for up to a quarter of an hour (+1.4 to +3.4 °C after switch-off was measured), which is why a plain thermostat overshoots on this plant.

Controllers. "On/off with OFF-anticipation" is the heater decision of the real firmware with its Config.h constants: ON when the projected temperature is below target − 0.2 °C; OFF when the projected temperature exceeds target + 0.2 − 0.1 °C (the anticipation); the projection is the reading plus the rate of rise over the last 60 s times a 200 s coast lead; a 30 s minimum OFF time; and after 600 s continuously ON a forced 60 s rest. The plain thermostat only uses the band. The PID is a PI tuned with Skogestad's SIMC rules on the first-order fit (kp 0.26 /°C, Ti 28 min), turned into relay pulses by time-proportioning over a 120 s window with a 10 s minimum pulse.

Safety task. As in the firmware, the interlock sits outside every controller: the heater is off while the door is open; a reading at or above 40 °C latches the heater off until an operator acknowledges and the chamber has cooled 0.5 °C; and when the sensor stops delivering valid readings for 10 s the heater is forced off and a sensor error is shown. The sensor reading handed to the controller is quantised to 0.01 °C like the SHT31 in the export.

What to try. Start cold with controller A = firmware and B = plain thermostat and watch the overshoot difference on the first approach. Set the anticipation and coast lead to zero to see the firmware logic degrade into the plain thermostat. Raise the target to 38.8 °C (the maximum the firmware allows) and look at the duty cycle: the real device held 38.8 °C at about 19 % duty. Open the door at steady state; the recovery takes 12–18 minutes after it closes. Scale the heater power up to 1.5 and watch the cutoff latch.
